# Methodology

This directory collects the testing and evaluative apparatus developed around the Romeo Protocol.

## Methodological development

The project moved through a sequence of increasingly rigorous controls:

1. **Initial Romeo derivation** — contemporary lexical reconstruction, prosodic orientation, character differentiation, annotation, and counting.
2. **Cross-play testing** — comedy, courtship, jealousy, tragedy, satire, and lyrical passages exposed differences in how slang behaves across dramatic registers.
3. **Nuance-Fidelity Extension** — speech acts, subtext, ambiguity, metaphor chains, dramatic irony, character idiolect, power/coercion, rhetoric, performance, and information-retention tests.
4. **Gemini-informed prosodic and tonal review** — natural-pronunciation-first reconstruction, beat-level gravity, tonal-deflation controls, and tiered contemporary registers.
5. **DRASP structural stress testing** — contradiction testing of claims about blank verse, fixed idioms, rhyme, performer variability, and modern lexical placement.
6. **Prose/pragmatic testing** — separation of structural elasticity from pragmatic and philosophical fidelity; temporal-localization and universality controls.

## Current modules

- **Module A — Prosodic Reconstruction Framework**
- **Module B — Dramatic Gravity and Tonal Fidelity Framework**
- **Module C — Prose Modality and Pragmatic Alignment Framework**

## Core methodological principle

A contemporary substitution is not successful merely because it is current, semantically clever, or metrically countable. It must be evaluated independently for dramatic function, character voice, rhetoric, prosody, pragmatic alignment, ambiguity, tonal gravity, universality, and performance.
