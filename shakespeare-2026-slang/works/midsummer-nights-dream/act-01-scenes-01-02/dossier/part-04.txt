# Revision Layer D - Comparison Against the Romeo Protocol

The user observed that the earlier Romeo and Juliet work sounded more thoroughly updated than the Midsummer drafts. That assessment was accepted.

The canonical Romeo artifact states that it consolidates three successive iambic-pentameter-oriented rewrites, retains Shakespeare's original dramatic sequence and recognizable Early Modern syntactic frame wherever possible, and bolds contemporary slang, digital-era lexicon, and wording rewritten to accommodate those substitutions. Its annotations distinguish close lexical equivalence from looser metaphorical or metrical accommodation. The user-supplied slang PDF is the primary slang source, with selected 2025-2026 dictionary sources used to corroborate slang absent from the glossary.

The gap was diagnosed as follows:

1. The Midsummer PDF-first rewrite treated the slang glossary too narrowly, almost as if only exact one-to-one matches were allowed.
2. The Romeo method allowed semantic and metaphorical substitution, not merely synonym replacement.
3. Romeo modernized the connective tissue around slang; the earlier Midsummer version left too much surrounding syntax in neutral modern English.
4. Romeo created recurring lexical systems: hard-launch, down bad, lore, cap/no cap, vibe, cook, ghost, etc. were reused and inflected across the scene.
5. Romeo had multiple revision passes, so it found second-order substitutions in imagery, jokes, interpersonal dynamics, digital metaphors, and meter.
6. Bottom's characterization especially supported a denser 2026 lexicon than the prior Midsummer version used.

The project therefore adopted this standing rule:

"All translation works produced together must, at a minimum, match the Romeo protocol."

The Romeo protocol is a floor, not a ceiling. It requires:
- Shakespearean dramatic structure and character differentiation preserved.
- Iambic-pentameter/prosodic orientation retained where feasible.
- Aggressive contemporary lexical reconstruction rather than sparse word replacement.
- The uploaded slang PDF used as the primary slang lexicon.
- Current slang absent from the PDF may be added only with separate contemporary support and clear labeling.
- Surrounding syntax may be rewritten to make slang function naturally.
- Slang and accommodation wording are bolded.
- Annotations identify source wording/function, rationale, equivalence strength, and metrical accommodation.
- Recurring slang forms coherent lexical systems.
- Scene-level and project-cumulative ledgers are maintained.

# Canonical Act I, Scene 1

THESEUS

Now, fair Hippolyta, our wedding hour
draws close; yet four days feel **lagging IRL**.
This moon is **hardstuck** inside her phase
and keeps my wants, like some old dowager
who leaves a young man's payout **paywalled**.

HIPPOLYTA

Four days will **speedrun** into four nights;
four nights will dream themselves away in haste.
Then shall the moon, bent like a silver bow,
look down upon the night we make this bond.

THESEUS

Go, Philostrate. Tell Athens **we outside**.
Wake every quick and nimble spirit up;
make melancholy **log off** till funerals.
Our wedding should be **peak**, not funeral-core.

[Philostrate exits.]

Hippolyta, our origin was **out of pocket**:
I wooed thee with a sword and won by harm.
But now we **hard-launch** in another key,
with pomp, with triumph, revelry, and joy.

[Enter EGEUS, HERMIA, LYSANDER, and DEMETRIUS.]

EGEUS

Long life to Theseus, our famous duke!

THESEUS

Thanks, Egeus. What news?

EGEUS

against my child, my daughter Hermia.
Demetrius, stand forth. My lord, this man
has my consent to marry her. Lysander,
stand forth. This man has worked his **rizz** on her,
has fed her rhymes and traded tokens too,
has staged a moonlit **thirst trap** at her window,
with fake-love verses, bracelets, rings, and gifts,
a whole romantic **soft launch** built to hook
a young and still-unhardened fantasy.
By cunning he has stolen her affection
and pushed my daughter toward a **villain era**,
turning obedience into stubbornness.

If she will not consent to marry him,
Demetrius, then Athens gives me **admin rights**:
as she is mine, I choose where she shall go-
to this man, or to death. That is the law.

THESEUS

What say you, Hermia? **Be for real**.
Your father, in your world, should rank as god.
He made the first **character build** of you;
you're wax impressed beneath his shaping hand,
and he may keep that figure or destroy it.
Demetrius reads plainly **green flag**.

So does Lysander.

THESEUS

Yes, himself he does,
but your own father does not **ship** that match.
His judgment therefore gives the other rank.

HERMIA

I wish my father looked but through my eyes.

THESEUS

Your eyes must learn to use his point of view.

HERMIA

Your Grace, forgive the boldness of my speech.
I know not how it plays before this court,
but tell me this, **no cap**: what is the worst,
the absolute **hard cap**, if I refuse
to marry with Demetrius?

THESEUS

Death-or **hard block** the company of men
forever. Hermia, therefore **lock in**.
Examine youth, desire, and what you want.
If you reject the choice your father made,
can you endure **nun-core** all your life,
shut in a cloister, childless, chanting hymns
unto the cold and unproductive moon?
Thrice blessed are they who master their desire,
yet earthlier happy is the rose distilled
than one that grows and withers on the thorn.

HERMIA

Then let me grow, and live, and die that way
before I **hard-launch** a man I do not choose
or grant him **admin access** to my soul.

THESEUS

Take time. By the next moon-the day I seal
my everlasting bond with Hippolyta-
you must **lock it in**: prepare to die,
or marry with Demetrius as commanded,
or vow austerity and single life.

DEMETRIUS

Relent, sweet Hermia. Lysander, **hold this L**.
Give up your broken title to my right.

LYSANDER

You have her father's love. **Weird flex**, my man.
Let me have Hermia's; you marry him.

EGEUS

Scorn all you like. Demetrius has my love.
She is my child, and all my right in her
I settle and estate on Demetrius.

LYSANDER

My birth is every bit as good as his,
my property as great, perhaps still more.
My love exceeds his love; and most of all,
Hermia loves me. Why deny my right?

And here comes the **receipts thread** on this man:
Demetrius made love to Helena,
won all her soul, left her **down bad for you**,
then **ghosted** that whole bond for Hermia.
She still adores this walking **red flag** here,
this spotted, shifting, inconsistent man.

THESEUS

I have heard that **tea** and meant to speak,
but wedding business crowded out my mind.
Demetrius and Egeus, come with me.
I owe you both a private **vibe check**.

And Hermia, prepare yourself to fit
your wishes to your father's will, or else
the law of Athens gives you up to death
or to a vow of single life. We cannot
soften the statute merely for your case.

Come, Hippolyta. Gentlemen, come too.
I have some wedding business for you both
and matters touching closely on yourselves.

With duty and desire, we follow you.

For want of rain; and I could give enough

HERMIA

**Big yikes**-too high a love chained down to low.

LYSANDER

HERMIA
