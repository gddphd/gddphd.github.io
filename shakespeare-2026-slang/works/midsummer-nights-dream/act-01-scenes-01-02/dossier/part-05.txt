Only your beauty. Wish that fault were mine.

HERMIA

Take comfort. Soon he will not **clock** my face.
Lysander and I mean to **ghost** this town.
Before I knew Lysander, Athens seemed
a paradise; but love has made the whole
bright city feel a **hellscape** unto me.

LYSANDER

Helena, here's the **lore dump** of our plan.
Tomorrow night, when moonlight fills the grass
with silver and conceals the flight of lovers,
we mean to slip through Athens' gates unseen.

HERMIA

And in the wood where often you and I
lay on the primrose beds for **girl therapy**,
emptying our hearts of counsel sweet,
Lysander and I meet, then turn our eyes
away from Athens toward another life,
new friends, new company, a different **era**.
This plan stays **close friends only**. Pray for us,
and good luck grant thee thy Demetrius.
Keep word, Lysander. Till tomorrow night,
our eyes must fast from lovers' company.

LYSANDER

I will, my Hermia.

[Hermia exits.]

Helena, farewell. May he get **down bad for you**
as deeply as thou still art down for him.

[Lysander exits.]

HELENA

How happy some can be beyond the rest.
All Athens says I'm every bit as fair
as Hermia, yet Demetrius calls me **mid**.
What matters public judgment if he won't
believe the thing that everyone can see?

As he mistakes Hermia for perfection,
I make the same mistake admiring him.
Love takes what's base and vile and gives a **glow up**,
turning no-worth to form and dignity.
Love looks not with the eyes but with the mind;
the **math ain't mathing**, therefore Cupid's blind.
His wings with no eyes picture reckless haste,
and childish Love gets fooled in every choice.
His whole selection process? **Peak delulu**.

Before Demetrius saw Hermia's eyes,
he rained down vows-**no cap**-that I was his.
Then Hermia brought heat, and every oath
got **hard-deleted** almost instantly.

So now for him it's **tea time**: I will tell
that Hermia plans tomorrow night to flee.
He'll chase her to the wood; and if I earn
one word of thanks, I purchase it with pain.
I know the move is deeply **down bad** still,
but I shall see him there and back. **We move**.

[Helena exits.]

# Canonical Act I, Scene 2

[Enter QUINCE, SNUG, BOTTOM, FLUTE, SNOUT, and STARVELING.]

Is all our company here? Are we **locked in**?

BOTTOM

Call every man by name from off the list.

Here is the scroll of every Athenian

Our play is called The Most Lamentable
Comedy and Most Cruel Death of Pyramus

BOTTOM

That title's **peak**. **No cap**, it already **slaps hard**.

Answer when called. Nick Bottom, weaver.

Here. Tell my role and keep the casting moving.

A lover who kills himself for love,

A lover? I can **cook and eat** that part.

they'd better guard their eyes. I'll move whole storms

The raging rocks,
the shivering shocks,
shall **cook** the locks
of prison gates;
and Phoebus' car

Francis Flute, the bellows-mender.

Here, Peter Quince.

The lady Pyramus must love.

Doesn't matter.

That is **clown behavior**. **Pack it up**.

**Say less**. Proceed.

Robin Starveling, tailor.

Here.

Here.

You play the father of our Pyramus.

There's no script.

The lion? **Bet**.

If you should roar too hard, you'll scare the Duchess
and all the ladies into shrieks, and then
they'll have us hanged.

**Big yikes**. Every one.

Agreed. But that's a simple **skill issue**.

At the Duke's Oak.

## Canonical Annotation and Equivalence Notes - Act I, Scene 1

### Opening temporal / celebration system

**Lagging IRL**, **hardstuck**, **paywalled**, and **speedrun** convert Theseus's complaint about time into a contemporary technological/gaming temporal register rather than merely paraphrasing "the moon is slow." "Speedrun" is attested in the uploaded glossary as completing a task as fast as possible. "Hardstuck" is attested as being stuck at the same rank; applying it to the moon is a controlled metaphorical transfer. Equivalence strength: moderate-to-strong metaphorical.

**We outside**, **log off**, **peak**, and **out of pocket** reconstruct Theseus's wedding register. "We outside" is attested as actively social and out. "Out of pocket" is attested as inappropriate or shocking behavior, making it a pointed modern gloss on Theseus's own admission that his original courtship involved warfare and injury. Equivalence strength: strong pragmatic for "we outside"; moderate interpretive for "out of pocket."

**Hard-launch** modernizes Theseus's shift from violent conquest to public wedding celebration. It also becomes a recurring relationship-status term elsewhere in the project. Equivalence strength: moderate-to-strong pragmatic.

### Egeus / Hermia / Theseus authority system

**Rizz**, **thirst trap**, and **soft launch** reconstruct Egeus's accusation that Lysander used songs, gifts, moonlight, and courtship performance to win Hermia. These are character-framed allegations; the adaptation does not independently establish manipulation as fact. Equivalence strength: moderate pragmatic/metaphorical.

**Villain era** gives Egeus a comic, biased description for Hermia's disobedience. The glossary describes "villain era" as prioritizing oneself over niceness. This is Egeus's framing, not an objective characterization. Equivalence strength: moderate pragmatic.

**Admin rights**, **character build**, and **admin access** are not asserted as slang. They are digital-era accommodations, analogous to the Romeo edition's deliberate use of contemporary objects or computing language where a literal slang synonym would not capture the controlling metaphor. They translate the ownership/control structure already present in Egeus and Theseus rather than softening it.

**Green flag**, **red flag**, and **ship** create a coherent relationship-evaluation system. The uploaded glossary supports green flag as a positive relationship indicator, red flag as a serious warning sign, and ship as support for a romantic pairing. Equivalence strength: strong functional.

**No cap**, **hard cap**, **hard block**, **lock in**, and **lock it in** move Hermia's legal questioning and Theseus's ultimatum into a current commitment/control register. "Hard cap" is especially close to Hermia's request for the maximum penalty. Equivalence strength ranges from strong lexical to moderate metaphorical.

**Nun-core** is a productive construction rather than a verbatim glossary entry. The glossary supports "core" as a suffix defining an aesthetic genre; the translation transfers that construction to a whole-life cloistered condition. Equivalence strength: loose/comic lexical accommodation, with the following lines preserving the severity of the actual choice.

### Rivalry / evidence system

**Hold this L**, **weird flex**, and **receipts thread** sharpen Demetrius-Lysander rivalry. "Hold this L" means accept your loss; "weird flex" means an odd boast; "receipts thread" means a collection of proof. Lysander's exposure of Demetrius's history with Helena is therefore rendered as evidentiary receipts rather than free-floating insult. Equivalence strength: strong pragmatic.

**Down bad for you**, **ghosted**, and **red flag** translate the Helena/Demetrius history into a current relationship lexicon. "Down bad for you" is especially close to Shakespeare's repeated "dotes." Equivalence strength: strong for down bad; moderate for ghosted.

**Tea** and **vibe check** shift Theseus's awareness and planned private admonition into contemporary information/social assessment language. Equivalence strength: moderate pragmatic.

### "The course of true love" system

The famous impediment catalogue receives a denser Romeo-style reconstruction. **Not giving**, **Big yikes**, **third-party**, **clock**, **ghost from sight**, **cooked**, **new meta**, **hard-blocked**, **Say less**, and **cap/no cap** translate class mismatch, age mismatch, external interference, sudden disappearance, recurring failure, strategic patience, escape, agreement, and truthfulness.
