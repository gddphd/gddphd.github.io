But now we'll **hard launch** this marriage differently:
with triumph, ceremony, feast, and dancing.

[Philostrate exits. Enter EGEUS with HERMIA, LYSANDER, and DEMETRIUS.]

EGEUS

Long life to Theseus, our famous duke!

THESEUS

Thanks, Egeus. What's the news?

EGEUS

I come **heated**, bringing my complaint
against my daughter Hermia. Demetrius-
this man has my consent to marry her.

Lysander-this one has worked on her heart.
He came with poems, moonlight songs, and **rizz**,
with bracelets, rings, sweet gifts, and little tokens.
He's got her **down bad**, lost inside **delulu**,
and changed the obedience she owed to me
into refusal.

If she will not marry
Demetrius here, I claim the law of Athens:
as she is mine, I choose where she shall go-
to this man, or to death.

THESEUS

Hermia, **be for real** and think this through.
Your father gave the form you carry now;
the law gives him the power to keep that form
or break it. In his view, Demetrius
is clearly a **green flag**.

HERMIA

So is Lysander.

THESEUS

Maybe in himself. Your father does not **ship**
the two of you, and here his voice prevails.

HERMIA

I wish my father saw him through my eyes.

THESEUS

Your eyes must learn to see through his judgment.

HERMIA

Your Grace, forgive me. I don't know what gives
me nerve to speak so freely in this court,
but tell me plainly: what is the **hard cap**
on punishment if I refuse Demetrius?

THESEUS

I won't disguise it. If you still refuse,
**You're cooked**: you die, or swear off men for life.

Consider youth, desire, and whether you
can live enclosed, a sister all your days,
singing cold hymns beneath the barren moon.

Blessed are those who master that desire,
but earthlier joy belongs to roses used,
not roses withering untouched on the thorn.

HERMIA

Then let me grow and wither on that thorn
before I yield myself to someone whom
my soul refuses as its sovereign.

THESEUS

You have until the next new moon-the day
Hippolyta and I are bound for life.

Then marry as your father orders you,
prepare to die, or choose perpetual chastity.

DEMETRIUS

Hermia, relent. Lysander, **Hold this L**.
Give up your broken claim to what is mine.

LYSANDER

Demetrius, **be so for real**. You already
have her father's love. Let me have hers.
If love is all you need, then marry him.

EGEUS

Mock if you like. Demetrius has my love,
and all my right in Hermia goes to him.

LYSANDER

My birth is every bit as good as his.
My wealth is equal, maybe even more.
My love is greater-and above all else,
Hermia loves me. Why deny my claim?

And here's a **fact-check** on Demetrius:
he courted Nedar's daughter Helena,
won all her heart, and now she's **down bad for you**,
while you switched targets. That's a **red flag**.

THESEUS

I've heard this story and meant to address it,
but wedding matters pushed it from my mind.

Demetrius, Egeus, come with me.
Hermia, prepare to bend your private wish
to what your father wills, or Athens' law
delivers you to death or single life.

Come, Hippolyta. You two, follow me.

EGEUS

With duty and desire.

[All but HERMIA and LYSANDER exit.]

LYSANDER

My love, why has your color left your face?
Why have the roses faded from your cheeks?

HERMIA

They need the rain, and I have rain enough
to give them from the storm behind my eyes.

LYSANDER

Every book I've read, each tale I've heard,
has taught me this: true love, **no cap**, has never
run down an easy road.

There's always rank,
or age, or friends who choose for someone else-

HERMIA

**Big yikes**: to choose your love through others' eyes.

LYSANDER

-or even when two people freely choose,
war, sickness, death can hit them without warning.
Bright love can vanish like a flash at night,
seen for one instant, swallowed up by dark.

HERMIA

If lovers always meet resistance, then
we'll treat it as the ordinary cost
of loving: thoughts and dreams and sighs and tears.
We'll learn to bear it.

LYSANDER

Good. Then hear my plan.

I have a wealthy widowed aunt with no
child of her own. She treats me like a son.
She lives beyond the reach of Athens' law.

Our **main quest** is simple: leave this city.

Tomorrow night, slip quietly from your home.
Meet me within the woods beyond the town,
where once you went with Helena in May.
I'll wait for you.

HERMIA

**Say less**. By Cupid's strongest bow,
by Venus, by the fire of ancient lovers,
by every vow that faithless men have broken,
I swear tomorrow I will meet you there.

LYSANDER

Keep your word, love. Look-here comes Helena.

[Enter HELENA.]

HERMIA

Good luck, Helena. Where are you going?

HELENA

Don't call me fair. Demetrius calls you fair.
Your eyes pull him; your voice can turn his heart.

If beauty could be caught like sickness, I'd
catch yours. Teach me your **rizz**. What do you do
that makes Demetrius follow where you lead?

HERMIA

I frown at him. He loves me anyway.

HELENA

I smile at him and get the opposite.

HERMIA

I curse him; still he answers me with love.

HELENA

I pray for love and still receive his hate.

HERMIA

The more I hate him, more he follows me.

HELENA

The more I love him, more he turns away.
I'm **down bad**, Hermia. That's the whole problem.

HERMIA

His foolishness is not a fault of mine.

HELENA

Only your beauty. Wish that fault were mine!

HERMIA

Take comfort. He won't see my face much longer.
Lysander and I leave Athens tomorrow.

Before I knew Lysander, this place felt
like paradise; now love has changed the place.
We'll leave.

LYSANDER

Helena, we're trusting you:
tomorrow night, beneath the moon, we'll pass
through Athens' gates and disappear unseen.

This is no **hard launch**; keep our plan concealed.

HERMIA

We'll meet out in the woods where you and I
once lay among the primroses and talked,
and from there turn away from Athens' life
to find new friends and unfamiliar homes.

Goodbye, my friend. Pray things work out for us,
and may Demetrius finally love you back.

Lysander, keep your promise.

LYSANDER

I will.

[Hermia exits.]

Farewell, Helena. May Demetrius love
you with the same devotion you give him.

[Lysander exits.]

HELENA

Some people get so much more luck than others.
All Athens says I'm every bit as fair
as Hermia; Demetrius disagrees.

Here's my **hot take**: love looks with the mind,
not with the eyes. That's why blind Cupid flies.
Love has no judgment; it moves far too fast.
It's basically **delulu** when it chooses.

Demetrius once swore, **no cap**, he was mine;
then Hermia appeared and every oath
melted away.

I know this looks **thirsty**,
but I'll go tell him Hermia plans to flee.
He'll chase her to the woods tomorrow night.

If telling him earns me one grateful look,
I'll pay the cost in pain. At least I'll see him.

**We move**.

[Helena exits.]

Pyramus.

## PDF-First Annotation Highlights

- **Rizz**: charisma/flirting skill. It mapped well onto Lysander's courting tactics and Helena's question about Hermia's attraction.
- **Down bad / down bad for you**: desperate or lovesick; especially strong for Helena's devotion.
- **Delulu**: delusional optimism/fantasy. Used as a speaker-framed characterization, not a narrator-endorsed diagnosis.
- **Green flag / red flag / ship**: direct relationship vocabulary for approval, warning, and pairing support.
- **Hard cap / You're cooked / Hold this L**: compressed legal and competitive stakes into strongly contemporary discourse.
- **Main quest / Say less / We move**: turned planning, agreement, and continuation into current social/gaming idiom.
