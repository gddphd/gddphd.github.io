# Revision Layer B - Initial Egeus / Hermia / Lysander / Demetrius Draft
## Act I, Scene 1: Egeus's Complaint Through Theseus's Departure

[Enter EGEUS with HERMIA, LYSANDER, and DEMETRIUS.]

EGEUS

Long life to Theseus, our famous duke!

THESEUS

Thanks, Egeus. What news do you bring me?

EGEUS

I come here **pressed**, with grievance in my chest
Against my child, my daughter Hermia.

Demetrius, step up. My noble lord,
This man has my consent to marry her.

Lysander, step up. And, gracious duke,
This man has **got in her head** and changed her.

You, Lysander-you've **love-bombed** her with rhymes
And traded little love gifts with my child.

At moonlight, underneath her window, you
Have sung those **fake-deep** verses about love
In some put-on, romantic lover's voice,

And **got her hooked** upon your whole persona
With hair-made bracelets, rings, bright little gifts,
Trinkets and flowers, candy-all that stuff
That **hits hard** on a young, unguarded heart.

By **playing slick**, you stole my daughter's heart
And turned the duty she should show to me
Into this **straight-up defiance**.

And, gracious duke, if she will not agree
Right here, before your Grace, to marry him-
Demetrius-I claim old Athens' law.

Because she's mine, the law lets me decide:
I give her either to this gentleman
Or give her over to her death, as law
Has already provided in this case.

THESEUS

What do you say, Hermia? Think carefully.

Your father, in your world, should rank as god:
He formed the beauty that you carry now.
You're like the wax on which he stamped a form,
And under law he claims the power to keep
That figure as it is-or disfigure it.

Demetrius is **a solid guy**.

HERMIA

So is Lysander.

THESEUS

Yes, considered on his own, he certainly is.
But here your father will not **co-sign** him,
And so the other must be ranked above.

HERMIA

I wish my father looked through my own eyes.

THESEUS

Your eyes must learn to look through his judgment.

HERMIA

Your Grace, I ask your pardon for my words.

I don't know where I found the nerve to speak,
Or whether speaking so before this court
Might make me look immodest or too bold.

But I ask only this: please tell me now
The worst that can be done to me by law
If I **hard-pass** on marrying Demetrius.

THESEUS

Either you die-or **swear off men** for life.

So, Hermia, examine what you want.
Remember you are young; **check yourself** well.
Ask whether, if you reject your father's choice,
You really can endure a life in **nun mode**:

Shut in a shadowed cloister all your life,
A childless sister, chanting weakened hymns
Up toward the cold and unproductive moon.

Three times blessed are those who master desire
And choose to make that maiden pilgrimage.

But earthly happiness, I tell you, favors
The rose distilled and rendered into sweetness
Above the rose that withers on its thorn
And grows, and lives, and dies entirely single.

HERMIA

Then let me grow, and live, and die that way
Before I **give him the keys** to any claim
Upon my body or my future life
When my own soul will grant him no such rule.

THESEUS

You have until the coming of the moon-
The day that seals Hippolyta and me
Together in our everlasting bond.

And on that day, prepare yourself to die
For disobeying what your father wills,

Or marry with Demetrius as he asks,

Or at Diana's altar **go full celibate**,
Committed to austerity for life.

DEMETRIUS

Relent, sweet Hermia. Lysander, **take the L**.
Give up your shaky title to my right.

LYSANDER

You have her father's love, Demetrius.
Let me have Hermia's. You marry him.

EGEUS

Mock all you want, Lysander. Yes, it's true:
Demetrius has my love, and what is mine
My love will freely render unto him.

And she is mine. Whatever rights I hold
In Hermia I settle upon him.

LYSANDER

My lord, my family stands as high as his.
My property is equal-maybe more.
My love for her is greater than his love,

And more important than those boasts combined:
The beautiful Hermia loves me back.

So why should I not press my rightful claim?

And I can **bring receipts** on Demetrius:

He courted Nedar's daughter, Helena.
He won her heart-and that poor woman still
Is **down bad** for him, utterly devoted,
Still worshipping this **walking red flag** here,
This inconsistent man who changes course.

THESEUS

I have to say, I have heard that before.

I meant to speak with Demetrius about it,
But wedding business crowded out my thought.

Demetrius, come. And Egeus, come with me.
You both require a private **reality check**.

And Hermia, prepare yourself to make
Your wishes fit the wishes of your father.

If not, the law of Athens hands you over-
And we're not authorized to soften it-
Either to death or lifelong single vows.

Come, Hippolyta. How are you, my love?

Demetrius and Egeus, come along.
I need you both concerning wedding business
And other matters touching you directly.

EGEUS

With duty and desire, we follow you.

[THESEUS, HIPPOLYTA, EGEUS, and DEMETRIUS exit. HERMIA and LYSANDER remain.]

# Revision Layer C - PDF-First Canonical Rewrite (Later Superseded)
## Methodological Change

The uploaded PDF "Social Media Slang: Emoji and Acronym Meanings Teens are Using" became the primary slang-reference authority. The project adopted a reference-status distinction:

| Reference status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| PDF-attested | Exact term/phrase appears in the uploaded glossary. |
| PDF-attested variant | A closely related construction appears, but the adaptation wording differs. |
| Supplemental contemporary usage | Not established from the PDF; separately supported where needed. |
| Creative adaptation | Constructed for the Shakespeare translation rather than asserted as an independently attested slang expression. |

The PDF itself describes slang as rapidly evolving and context-dependent, and reports 864 glossary entries across emoji, acronym, and phrase categories. The project therefore stopped treating general modern English as slang and began differentiating evidentiary status.

## Act I, Scene 1 - PDF-First Rewrite

[Enter THESEUS, HIPPOLYTA, PHILOSTRATE, and attendants.]

THESEUS

Hippolyta, four days until we're wed,
but this old moon is **lagging IRL** for me.
She keeps me waiting on the thing I want,
like some rich dowager who won't let go
while younger heirs keep waiting on the cash.

HIPPOLYTA

Four days will fold themselves into four nights;
four nights will dream themselves away soon enough.
Then that new moon, bent like a silver bow,
will watch the night we make our marriage public.

THESEUS

Philostrate, go tell the youth of Athens:
**We outside**. Wake every joyful spirit.
Send melancholy where the funerals are;
this wedding celebration **slaps hard**.

Hippolyta, I courted you through war
and won you while I did you injury.
