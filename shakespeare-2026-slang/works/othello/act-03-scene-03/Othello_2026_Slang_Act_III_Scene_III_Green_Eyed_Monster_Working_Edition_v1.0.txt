2. CONTROLLING EDITORIAL PROTOCOL - ROMEO PROTOCOL
======================================================================

All Shakespeare-to-2026-slang translations in this project must, at minimum, match the Romeo and Juliet protocol.

The controlling requirements are:

1. Preserve Shakespeare's dramatic sequence, character relationships, argumentative structure, and recognizable rhetorical architecture.
2. Maintain an iambic-pentameter / Shakespearean prosodic orientation where feasible; do not falsely claim mechanical ten-syllable regularity where the line does not sustain it.
3. Use aggressive contemporary lexical reconstruction rather than sparse slang insertion.
4. Use the user-supplied slang PDF as the primary slang lexicon.
5. Permit separately corroborated contemporary slang when the PDF does not contain a needed term, but label that evidentiary status clearly.
6. Allow surrounding syntax to be rewritten when necessary so slang feels structurally integrated rather than pasted into archaic prose.
7. Bold slang and wording materially rewritten to accommodate the contemporary lexical layer.
8. Annotate each major lexical intervention by:
   - Shakespearean source/function,
   - 2026 replacement,
   - semantic rationale,
   - equivalence strength,
   - source status,
   - metrical/prosodic accommodation where relevant.
9. Maintain coherent recurring lexical systems across scenes and works.
10. Maintain scene-level and cumulative project-wide counts.
11. Preserve revision history and superseded versions rather than silently erasing them.
12. Distinguish slang, slang-derived variants, supplemental contemporary slang, and non-slang digital-era accommodation.

This protocol is a floor, not a ceiling.

4. EDITORIAL SCOPE OF THIS OTHELLO PASS
======================================================================

This working edition translates the famous central jealousy exchange in Act III, Scene III.

The adapted sequence includes:
- Iago's claim that reputation is valuable.
- Othello's demand that Iago speak plainly.
- Iago's warning about jealousy and the "green-eyed monster."
- The misery of uncertain suspicion.
- Othello's insistence that he will not live by mere suspicion.
- Othello's stated requirement to see before doubting and demand proof once doubt arises.
- Iago's continued insinuation while withholding proof.

The purpose of this pass is not to modernize every line in the full scene. It creates a canonical Romeo-protocol adaptation of this famous unit for later expansion.

5. OTHELLO, ACT III, SCENE III
2026 LEXICAL LAYER / SHAKESPEAREAN PROSODY
======================================================================

OTHELLO

What mean'st thou? **Be for real** and speak it plain.

IAGO

Good name in man and woman, noble lord,
Is social capital—their **clout**, their trust.
Who steals my purse steals trash; cash comes and goes.
But steal my name, and thou hast **hard-deleted**
What profits thee no whit, yet ruins me.

OTHELLO

By heaven, I will **fact-check** every thought.

IAGO

You cannot, were my heart within your hand;
Nor shall I **lore dump** what I keep in mine.

OTHELLO

Ha?

IAGO

O, **vibe check**, my lord: beware of jealousy!
The green-eyed monster is a **toxic** feed
That **gaslights** what it eats, then feeds again.
The man who knows betrayal may **hard block**
The love that wronged him and at least know peace;
But damned are minutes in a **doomscrolling** loop,
Still **down bad**, still doubting, everything looks **sus**,
Still loving hard the one he half suspects.

OTHELLO

O misery!

IAGO

Poor and content is rich, and rich enough;
But endless wealth feels winter-poor to him
Who fears each hour that everything may vanish.
Good God defend us from that **toxic** state!

OTHELLO

Why, why is this? Think'st thou I'd make doubt
My **main quest**, moon by moon, with fresh **sus** threads?
No. One real doubt and I **fact-check** the claim.
Trade me for some goat—not **GOAT**, a goat—
The day I build my soul on **unverified** smoke
That matches only **source: trust me bro**.

To say my wife is **fire**, loves company,
Speaks freely, sings and dances—none is **sus**.
Where virtue lives, those traits are **green flags**.
Nor will I call my worth a **skill issue**
And fear she'll switch; she had her eyes and chose me.

I'll **clock** before I doubt; when doubt arrives,
Bring **receipts or it didn't happen**. Then,
Upon the proof, there is no middle state:
Either my love—or jealousy—gets **hard-deleted**.

IAGO

I am glad of this. Then I can speak more freely,
But mark me: what I say is still **allegedly**.
I am not dropping **receipts** yet. Watch your wife;
Observe her well with Cassio. Keep your eyes
Neither **delulu** nor asleep to **red flags**.

6. LEXICAL ANNOTATION REGISTER
======================================================================

| ID | 2026 intervention | Shakespearean function | Classification | Evidence / rationale |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| OTH-001 | **Be for real** | Othello orders Iago to speak plainly and sincerely. | Strong pragmatic equivalence | Contemporary demand for seriousness / honesty. Functions as a direct discourse-marker modernization. |
| OTH-002 | **clout** | Iago's "good name" / reputation. | Moderate-to-strong equivalence | Clout means influence or social capital. It closely fits reputation's social value, though Shakespeare's "good name" also carries moral honor beyond social capital. |
| OTH-003 | **hard-deleted / hard-deleted** | Reputation destroyed; later either love or jealousy must be eliminated. | Strong digital-functional analogy | Hard delete denotes permanent removal. Reuse establishes a coherent deletion lexical system. |
| OTH-004 | **fact-check** | Othello insists suspicion must be tested against evidence. | Strong functional equivalence | Fact-checking maps directly onto verification of claims. Repetition reinforces Othello's stated epistemic standard. |
| OTH-005 | **lore dump** | Iago refuses to disclose what he claims to know. | Moderate pragmatic equivalence | Lore dump means a large disclosure of backstory/information. Used negatively here: Iago refuses the disclosure Othello demands. |
| OTH-006 | **vibe check** | Iago pivots from withholding to warning Othello about jealousy. | Moderate pragmatic equivalence | Vibe check denotes assessing atmosphere or behavior. It modernizes Iago's diagnostic pose. |
| OTH-007 | **toxic** | Jealousy as harmful, corrosive state. | Strong pragmatic equivalence | Toxic denotes harmful negative behavior/state. The term retains the destructive moral-psychological force of jealousy. |
| OTH-008 | **gaslights** | Jealousy distorts perception and feeds on its own distortions. | Moderate metaphorical equivalence | Gaslighting concerns manipulation of perceived reality. Here jealousy itself is personified as the agent of perceptual distortion. |
| OTH-009 | **hard block** | Known betrayal may permit decisive severance. | Moderate-to-strong digital analogy | Hard block means complete blocking. It translates decisive termination of a harmful relationship into digital exclusion language. |
| OTH-010 | **doomscrolling** | "What damned minutes tells he o'er" / repetitive jealous rumination. | Strong psychological metaphor | Doomscrolling describes endless negative-information consumption. It maps well onto repetitive, self-reinforcing rumination while changing the original imagery. |
| OTH-011 | **down bad** | Lover remains emotionally attached while suffering suspicion. | Moderate pragmatic equivalence | Down bad denotes desperate/lovesick attachment. Fits the persistence of desire despite jealousy. |
| OTH-012 | **sus** | Suspicion, doubt, questionable appearances. | Strong lexical equivalence | Sus directly means suspicious/questionable. Repetition forms the scene's compact suspicion marker. |
| OTH-013 | **main quest** | Othello rejects making jealousy/doubt his central preoccupation. | Strong metaphorical equivalence | Main quest denotes a primary life goal/task. Othello insists doubt will not become his organizing purpose. |
| OTH-014 | **GOAT** | Shakespeare's literal "Exchange me for a goat." | Creative wordplay / accommodation | The acronym GOAT ("greatest of all time") collides with Shakespeare's literal animal. "Not GOAT, a goat" preserves the animal while creating a deliberate 2026 joke. |
| OTH-015 | **unverified** | Othello rejects unsupported suspicion. | Strong epistemic modernization | Unverified marks a claim lacking confirmation. It preserves Othello's refusal to treat conjecture as proof. |
| OTH-016 | **source: trust me bro** | Unsupported insinuation posing as knowledge. | Strong epistemic/comic analogy | A joking phrase for an unreliable citation. It makes Iago's evidentiary weakness explicit in contemporary information-literacy language. |
| OTH-017 | **fire** | Desdemona is attractive/lively/sociable. | Moderate evaluative equivalence | Fire signals excellence / attractiveness / excitement. It modernizes positive valuation without itself proving infidelity. |
| OTH-018 | **green flags** | Othello treats Desdemona's traits as compatible with virtue. | Strong relationship-language equivalence | Green flags are positive relationship indicators. The term preserves Othello's argument that these traits are not incriminating. |
| OTH-019 | **skill issue** | Othello rejects construing his own worth as the reason Desdemona might leave him. | Moderate comic metaphor | Skill issue assigns failure to lack of ability. Othello rejects that self-deprecating frame. |
| OTH-020 | **clock** | "I'll see before I doubt." | Strong colloquial analogue | Clock means notice/recognize. Carried forward from the Romeo lexical system to preserve cross-work consistency. |
| OTH-021 | **receipts or it didn't happen** | Othello demands proof before acting. | Strong functional equivalence | The phrase directly demands evidence for a claim, closely matching the scene's proof standard. |
| OTH-022 | **allegedly** | Iago presents insinuation as not yet proved. | Strong epistemic equivalence | Allegedly flags an unconfirmed claim, highlighting the gap between insinuation and proof. |
| OTH-023 | **receipts** | Documentary/evidentiary proof. | Strong functional equivalence | Receipts denotes evidence supporting an allegation. It forms a coherent evidence system with fact-check and unverified. |
| OTH-024 | **delulu** | Iago pretends to warn Othello against irrational interpretation. | Moderate derived use | Delulu denotes delusional fantasy/optimism; here broadened to irrational interpretation. It is not a literal synonym for jealousy. |
| OTH-025 | **red flags** | Iago encourages suspicious reading of Desdemona/Cassio. | Strong relationship-language equivalence | Red flags denote serious warning signs. The manipulation lies in Iago's attempt to make neutral conduct look incriminating. |

8. PROSODIC / METER NOTES
======================================================================

The adaptation follows the Romeo Protocol's prosodic principle:
- orient toward five-beat Shakespearean movement,
- preserve rhetorical architecture,
- permit inversions, contractions, substitutions, feminine endings, shared lines, and deliberate slang stress,
- do not falsely claim mechanical iambic pentameter where it is not sustained.

Illustrative lines:

What MEAN'ST | thou? BE | for REAL | and SPEAK | it PLAIN.

By HEAV'N | I WILL | fact-CHECK | each THOUGHT.

But DAMNED | are MIN | utes IN | a DOOM | scrolling LOOP.

I'll CLOCK | before | I DOUBT; | when DOUBT | arrives.

The slang often carries useful stress:
- FACT-check
- DOOM-scroll-ing
- HARD block
- GREEN flags
- RED flags
- MAIN quest

The objective is prosodic plausibility and rhetorical energy, not mechanical syllable counting.
