2. CONTROLLING EDITORIAL PROTOCOL - ROMEO PROTOCOL
======================================================================

All Shakespeare-to-2026-slang translations in this project must, at minimum, match the Romeo and Juliet protocol.

The controlling requirements are:

1. Preserve Shakespeare's dramatic sequence, character relationships, argumentative structure, and recognizable rhetorical architecture.
2. Maintain an iambic-pentameter / Shakespearean prosodic orientation where feasible; do not falsely claim mechanical ten-syllable regularity where the line does not sustain it.
3. Use aggressive contemporary lexical reconstruction rather than sparse slang insertion.
4. Use the user-supplied slang PDF as the primary slang lexicon.
5. Permit separately corroborated contemporary slang when the PDF does not contain a needed term, but label that evidentiary status clearly.
6. Allow surrounding syntax to be rewritten when necessary so slang feels structurally integrated rather than pasted into archaic prose.
7. Bold slang and wording materially rewritten to accommodate the contemporary lexical layer.
8. Annotate each major lexical intervention by:
   - Shakespearean source/function,
   - 2026 replacement,
   - semantic rationale,
   - equivalence strength,
   - source status,
   - metrical/prosodic accommodation where relevant.
9. Maintain coherent recurring lexical systems across scenes and works.
10. Maintain scene-level and cumulative project-wide counts.
11. Preserve revision history and superseded versions rather than silently erasing them.
12. Distinguish slang, slang-derived variants, supplemental contemporary slang, and non-slang digital-era accommodation.

This protocol is a floor, not a ceiling.

4. EDITORIAL SCOPE OF THIS KING LEAR PASS
======================================================================

This working edition translates Act III, Scene II, the storm scene.

The adaptation retains:
- Lear's apostrophe to the elements.
- His wish that the storm destroy nature's molds.
- The Fool's practical insistence that shelter would be wiser.
- Lear's distinction between the innocent storm and his ungrateful daughters.
- Kent's entrance and alarm at the weather.
- Lear's invocation of hidden criminals and concealed guilt.
- "I am a man / more sinned against than sinning."
- Lear's admission that his mind is beginning to fail.
- Lear's growing compassion for the Fool.
- The Fool's satirical prophecy.

The scene is used as a stress test of the Romeo Protocol because it combines high poetic rhetoric, imperatives, emotional escalation, iconic lines, philosophical language, comedy, and social satire.

5. KING LEAR, ACT III, SCENE II
2026 LEXICAL LAYER / SHAKESPEAREAN PROSODY
======================================================================

[Storm still. Enter LEAR and FOOL.]

LEAR

Blow, winds, and crack your cheeks! Go **feral**, blow!
You cataracts and hurricanes, start **maxxing**
your flood till steeples drown beneath the surge.
You sulphurous, thought-executing fires,
go **speedrun** thunder through the splitting oak;
singe this white head. And thou, world-shaking thunder,
come **hard reset** the roundness of the earth.
Crack nature's molds; spill every seed at once
that breeds ungrateful man. **Pack it up**, mankind.

FOOL

Nuncle, a dry house beats this outdoor **shitshow**.
Go ask thy daughters' blessing. **We inside**
is wiser than standing here while heaven **maldes**.
This night's got no respect for fool or king.

LEAR

Rumble thy bellyful! Spit fire! Spout rain!
Go **full feral**; do all thy worst to me.
Rain, wind, and thunder are not my two daughters.
I charge you not with being **out of pocket**.
I gave you no kingdom, called you not my children;
you owe me no subscription, no loyalty.
Then drop your horrible pleasure. Here I stand,
**deplatformed**, poor, infirm, weak, despised,
an old man getting **ratioed** by the sky.
Yet still I call you servile ministers,
for joining with my daughters' **villain era**
to wage your high-born war against this head,
so old and white. O, this is foul indeed!

FOOL

He that has a house to hide his head
has made at least one **clutch** decision.
The man who puts his toe where heart should go
has got a plain old **skill issue**, nuncle;
his corn will keep him waking half the night.
And every pretty face will **vibe check** mirrors.

LEAR

No. I will **lock in** as all patience.
I will say nothing.

[Enter KENT in disguise.]

KENT

Who's there?

FOOL

Here's grace and foolishness in one wet feed:
a wise man and a fool, both **cooked** by rain.

KENT

Alas, my lord, are you still here? The things
that love the dark don't love a night like this.
The wrathful heavens are truly **out of pocket**;
even night-creatures **hard block** this weather
and hide themselves in caves. Since I was man,
such sheets of fire, such thunder, wind, and rain
I never heard. No human frame can **hard-carry**
this much affliction and this much fear.

LEAR

Then let the gods who run this dreadful storm
go **mask off** now and find their enemies.
Tremble, you wretch whose undisclosed crimes
have dodged all justice. Hide, you bloody hand;
you liar dressed as virtue; hide, incestuous man.
You murderer beneath convenient smiles,
your secret **receipts incoming** tonight.
Let pent-up guilt break open every cover;
now **receipts dropped** expose each hidden crime.
Let every hidden fraud face **reality check**.

I am a man
more sinned against than sinning.

KENT

My lord-bareheaded?
There is a hovel close at hand. It's **mid**,
but still it offers shelter from the storm.
Rest there while I return to that hard house,
harder than all the stone that built its walls.
They shut me out when I came asking for you.
I'll go again and press their courtesy.

LEAR

My wits begin **lagging IRL**. Come, boy.
How dost thou, boy? Art cold? I am cold too.
Where is this straw? Necessity is strange:
it makes the lowest things turn **clutch** at once.
Come, lead us to the hovel. Poor Fool, poor knave,
one piece within my heart still feels for thee.

FOOL [sings]

He that has but a little tiny wit,
with hey, ho, the wind and the rain,
must **we move** through whatever fortune sends,
though rain keeps raining every day.

LEAR

True, my good boy. Come, bring us to the hovel.

[LEAR and KENT exit.]

FOOL

This is a brave night to cool anybody.
I'll drop one prophecy before I go.

When priests just **yap**, with little truth in practice,
when brewers sell us **shrinkflation** in beer,
when nobles **backseat** every tailor's craft,
and only lovers are the ones getting **cooked**;
when every lawsuit somehow comes out right,
and every squire has finally cleared his debt;
when slander quits **clout chasing** on each tongue,
and pickpockets **log off** from crowded streets;
when usurers **touch grass** counting gold,
and brothel keepers start constructing churches-
then Albion will hit a **vibe shift** so wild
the whole realm enters **peak delulu**.

Then comes the age, if anyone shall see it,
when people use their feet to walk again.

Merlin will post this prophecy much later;
I'm merely giving **early access** now.

[FOOL exits.]

8. ICONIC-LINE PRESERVATION RULE
======================================================================

The following line is deliberately preserved almost unchanged:

"I am a man
more sinned against than sinning."

Reason:
The Romeo Protocol requires aggressive modernization, but it does not require replacing every famous Shakespearean formulation. The stronger editorial choice here is to modernize the rhetorical machinery leading into the line and let the canonical declaration land without slang interference.

This establishes an additional practical rule:
When an iconic Shakespeare line retains exceptional rhetorical force in current English, preservation may be preferable to substitution if the surrounding passage already carries the 2026 lexical layer.

9. PROSODIC / METER NOTES
======================================================================

The adaptation follows the Romeo Protocol's prosodic principle:
- orient toward five-beat Shakespearean movement;
- retain imperatives, parallelism, catalogues, antitheses, and repeated rhetorical structures;
- permit inversions, contractions, substitutions, feminine endings, shared lines, and deliberate slang stress;
- do not claim mechanical iambic pentameter where the slang or syntax does not sustain it.

Illustrative stress-bearing lines:

Blow, WINDS | and CRACK | your CHEEKS | go FER | al, BLOW.

go SPEED | run THUN | der THROUGH | the SPLIT | ting OAK.

No. I | will LOCK | in AS | all PA | tience.

your SE | cret RE | ceipts IN | comING | toNIGHT.

The Fool's speech is intentionally looser and more conversational than Lear's.
Kent remains comparatively slang-light because his role is stabilizing, practical, and observational.

Character-specific lexical density is therefore part of the protocol:
- Lear: elevated rhetoric + violent modern intensifiers.
- Fool: highest comic/slang density.
- Kent: restrained, functional modernization.
