| Does the slang introduce an unintended social stance? | No unplanned irony, cynicism, mockery, self-branding, detachment, or platform logic is added. |
| Does the new phrasing preserve the target audience of the speech? | A universal or courtly observation is not accidentally narrowed to an online subculture unless intentionally justified. |

A phrase can therefore be structurally effortless, contemporarily authentic, and still fail the protocol because its pragmatic function is wrong.

## 58. Temporal Localization and Universality Preservation Gate

Highly specific contemporary vocabulary can transform a broad or archetypal observation into commentary tied to one platform, economic fad, meme cycle, or historical instant. Revision D treats this as a distinct fidelity risk.

| Localization level | Description | Typical effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| T0 - Transhistorical | Contemporary wording remains broadly intelligible and does not depend on a narrow platform or fad. | Low universality loss. |
| T1 - Contemporary-general | Clearly modern but not strongly tied to one platform or short-lived meme. | Usually acceptable across G1-G4 with normal checks. |
| T2 - Era-specific | Strongly associated with 2020s internet culture or a particular social behavior. | Can enrich comic/social specificity but may narrow philosophical reach. |
| T3 - Hyper-local / meme-bound | Depends on a short-lived trend, platform mechanic, or highly specific online grievance. | High risk in philosophical, prophetic, lyrical, or sublime material. |

### 58.1 Universality Question

Does the modernization preserve the breadth of the original insight, or does it convert a durable human/social observation into a narrowly 2026 complaint?

If the latter, test a broader Tier B, Tier C, or Tier D reconstruction before retaining the high-localization term.

## 59. Philosophical Compression Rule

When a Shakespearean passage generalizes about human nature, moral order, social corruption, mortality, authority, fate, or historical recurrence, the translation must not reduce that conceptual field merely because a contemporary expression offers an easy topical analogue.

The editor should record:

- Original scope: immediate / interpersonal / institutional / societal / existential / metaphysical.

- Modern scope after adaptation.

- Any narrowing caused by platform-specific, economic, or meme terminology.

- Whether the narrowing is intentional and dramatically justified.

- A broader alternative when the loss is not justified.

## 60. Structural Elasticity vs. Semantic Elasticity Distinction

| Dimension | Meaning | Failure example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Structural elasticity | How freely syntax, stress, length, and conversational rhythm can accommodate contemporary wording. | Blank verse may be structurally constrained; prose is usually more elastic. |
| Semantic/pragmatic elasticity | How much register change the dramatic thought can tolerate without changing scope, tone, archetype, or social function. | Philosophical prose may be structurally elastic but semantically inelastic to highly localized meme language. |

The Romeo Protocol must never infer semantic success from structural ease.

## 61. High-Density Prose Opportunity Rule

Transactional, logistical, ego-driven, and combative prose may be used as high-density contemporary-lexicon environments when the modern slang shares the scene's communicative goals.

This is an opportunity rule, not a quota. It does not authorize:

- increasing slang merely because a passage is prose;

- flattening differences among prose-speaking characters;

- replacing philosophical breadth with topical jokes;

- selecting scenes primarily because they permit more slang;

- treating prose as exempt from the Nuance Fidelity Stack, gravity system, ambiguity rule, or power/coercion checks.

## 62. Additional Multiaxial Scoring Dimensions

Revision D adds two diagnostic axes to the v2.0 multiaxial scoring system:

| Dimension | Score | Diagnostic question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pragmatic alignment | 0-5 | Does the contemporary expression perform the same communicative/social function as the Shakespearean source? |
| Universality preservation | 0-5 | Does the adaptation preserve the source's intended breadth across immediate, social, philosophical, or archetypal levels? |

These scores do not replace semantic, dramatic, character, figurative, rhetorical, prosodic, contemporary-authenticity, or ambiguity scores; they are cumulative.
