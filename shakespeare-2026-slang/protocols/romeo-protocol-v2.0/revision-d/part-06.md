## 56. Module C - Prose Modality and Pragmatic Alignment Framework

Purpose: distinguish structural freedom from semantic fitness. Prose may remove metrical constraints while still failing if the contemporary register performs a different social, philosophical, or rhetorical function from the source.

### 56.1 Controlling Principle

Prose elasticity is a necessary but not sufficient condition for successful contemporary lexical integration.

The editor must test not only whether slang fits the sentence naturally, but whether the slang's native pragmatic purpose matches the Shakespearean speech act and dramatic scope.

### 56.2 Evidence-Supported Prose Modalities

| Modality | Primary communicative function | Observed slang compatibility | Default editorial posture |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| P1 - Transactional / logistical prose | Assign roles, negotiate tasks, solve immediate problems, bargain, organize action. | Very high structural and pragmatic compatibility in the tested Midsummer mechanicals material. | Tier A-B language may be dense when character idiolect and gravity permit. |
| P2 - Ego-driven / combative social prose | Boast, mock, challenge, compete for status, interrupt, assert dominance. | Very high compatibility when the modern lexicon shares the same status-seeking or argumentative function. | High slang density may be appropriate; preserve power relations and comic timing. |
| P3 - Philosophical / diagnostic prose | Interpret human conduct, suffering, morality, society, or existential conditions. | Structurally easy but pragmatically high-risk when meme vocabulary narrows broad observation into platform-specific commentary. | Prefer Tier B-D; test universality and temporal-localization cost. |
| P4 - Prophetic / satirical-social prose | Generalize beyond the immediate scene; pronounce on society, history, institutions, or future conditions. | High risk of temporal over-localization despite syntactic freedom. | Use contemporary specifics selectively; preserve archetypal reach and multi-era legibility. |
| P? - Untested prose modality | Any prose function not sufficiently represented in the current corpus. | Unresolved. | Do not infer compatibility from prose form alone; run the Pragmatic Alignment Gate. |

P1-P4 are editorial categories derived from the tested contrast and are not claims that Shakespearean prose can be exhaustively classified into only four types. New modalities may be added after corpus testing.

## 57. Pragmatic Alignment Gate

For every high-salience slang intervention in prose, compare the source utterance's communicative purpose with the contemporary expression's normal social use.

| Gate question | Pass condition |
| --- | --- |
| What is the Shakespearean speaker trying to accomplish? | Speech act and pragmatic objective are explicitly identified. |
| What is the contemporary expression normally used to accomplish? | Its native pragmatic function is described from evidence or established project usage. |
| Do those functions align? | Strong or defensible functional overlap exists. |
