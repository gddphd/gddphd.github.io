## 1. Version-Preservation Statement

Romeo Protocol v2.0 is built cumulatively from the complete previously documented Romeo Protocol baseline. The twelve controlling requirements below are preserved in substance and wording. The Nuance-Fidelity Extension adds new requirements after that baseline rather than silently altering or deleting it.

No prior rule is deprecated by this draft unless a later user-authorized version explicitly identifies the rule and the authorized change.

## 2. Preserved Romeo Protocol Baseline

All Shakespeare-to-2026-slang translations in this project must, at minimum, match the Romeo and Juliet protocol.

The controlling requirements are:

1. Preserve Shakespeare's dramatic sequence, character relationships, argumentative structure, and recognizable rhetorical architecture.

2. Maintain an iambic-pentameter / Shakespearean prosodic orientation where feasible; do not falsely claim mechanical ten-syllable regularity where a line does not sustain it.

3. Use aggressive contemporary lexical reconstruction rather than sparse slang insertion.

4. Use the user-supplied slang PDF as the primary slang lexicon.

5. Permit separately corroborated contemporary slang when the PDF does not contain a needed term, but label that evidentiary status clearly.

6. Allow surrounding syntax to be rewritten when necessary so slang feels structurally integrated rather than pasted into archaic prose.

7. Bold slang and wording materially rewritten to accommodate the contemporary lexical layer.

8. Annotate each major lexical intervention by:

  - Shakespearean source/function,

  - 2026 replacement,

  - semantic rationale,

  - equivalence strength,

  - source status,

  - metrical/prosodic accommodation where relevant.

9. Maintain coherent recurring lexical systems across scenes and works.

10. Maintain scene-level and cumulative project-wide counts.

11. Preserve revision history and superseded versions rather than silently erasing them.

12. Distinguish slang, slang-derived variants, supplemental contemporary slang, and non-slang digital-era accommodation.

This protocol is a floor, not a ceiling.

## 3. Governing Objective of v2.0

The v2.0 objective is:

Reconstruct Shakespeare's dramatic, rhetorical, emotional, figurative, and prosodic functions inside a defensible contemporary linguistic system, using contemporary slang as one instrument rather than the sole objective.

The earlier objective of translating Shakespeare into 2026 slang while preserving Shakespearean meter remains valid, but v2.0 clarifies that lexical replacement is subordinate to dramatic-function fidelity.

## 4. Nuance Fidelity Stack

Before rewriting a significant line, speech, exchange, or rhetorical unit, evaluate the following layers. A translation may modernize any layer only if the resulting wording preserves or explicitly documents changes to the others.

| Layer | Required question |
| --- | --- |
| Denotative meaning | What is literally being said? |
| Speech act | What is the speaker doing with the utterance: threatening, seducing, deflecting, commanding, flattering, lying, pleading, mocking, testing, bargaining, consoling, etc.? |
| Emotional state | What is the speaker feeling, suppressing, or performing? |
| Power relationship | Who has authority, leverage, dependency, or vulnerability? |
| Subtext | What is communicated without being stated directly? |
| Rhetorical function | What argumentative or dramatic job does the line perform? |
| Figurative structure | What metaphor, symbol, or image carries the thought? |
| Ambiguity | Does the original deliberately support multiple meanings? |
| Dramatic irony | What does the audience know that the speaker does not? |
| Prosody | How do meter, stress, pause, rhyme, repetition, and sound contribute? |
| Character voice | Would this speaker plausibly use the proposed modern vocabulary? |
| Plot consequence | What later action or inference depends on the precise force of this line? |

## 5. Speech-Act Classification Requirement

For significant dialogue, identify at least:

- Literal statement.

- Operative speech act.

- Apparent intention.

- Actual or concealed intention, when supported by the play.

- Dramatic function.

Where the concealed intention is unavailable to the speaking character but available to the audience, the translation must preserve that asymmetry rather than inserting modern terminology that gives the speaker forbidden knowledge.

## 6. Ambiguity Preservation Rule

If Shakespeare's wording permits multiple plausible meanings, the translation should preserve equivalent ambiguity whenever reasonably possible.

Annotations for materially ambiguous lines must record one of the following:

- Original ambiguity A/B; modern version preserves A/B.

- Original ambiguity A/B; modern version preserves A and partially preserves B.

- Original ambiguity A/B; modern version narrows to A; loss recorded.

- Original ambiguity cannot be reproduced in contemporary wording; original ambiguity retained in annotation.

Sexual, political, theological, legal, class-based, and literal/figurative double meanings receive heightened review.

## 7. Metaphor-Chain Preservation Rule

When Shakespeare develops an image family over several lines, preserve the chain as a system rather than replacing each local image with unrelated slang.

| Original image family | Possible modern family | Editorial principle |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gardening / ripening | Cooking / development | Preserve growth, maturation, and outcome relationships. |
| Navigation | GPS / routing | Preserve direction, error, destination, and agency. |
| Disease | Viral / contamination | Preserve spread, infection, vulnerability, and cure logic. |
| Clothing / livery | Drip / aesthetic | Preserve dress, identity, rank, and appearance. |
| Warfare | Gaming / combat | Preserve opposition, attack, defense, victory, and defeat. |
| Reputation | Social-platform status | Preserve recognition, influence, damage, and public judgment. |

Each scene with a sustained figurative system should include a Metaphor Ledger.

## 8. Character Idiolect Matrix

Major characters should receive a reusable linguistic profile before or during translation. At minimum record:

- Slang density range.

- Register and education/status profile.

- Preferred semantic fields.

- Terms or constructions the character should avoid.

- Degree of Early Modern syntax retained.

- Typical sentence length and rhetorical complexity.

- Humor, irony, profanity, intimacy, and aggression tolerances.

Illustrative profiles from the current corpus:

| Character | Slang density | Register | Preferred semantic fields |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Romeo | High | lyrical / earnest | relationship, aesthetic, digital identity |
| Juliet | Medium-high | intelligent / direct | commitment, truth, privacy, identity |
| Iago | Medium | controlled / manipulative | evidence, suspicion, information |
| Othello | Medium | formal / declarative | proof, honor, judgment |
| Lear | Medium | elevated / explosive | destruction, systems failure, status |
| Fool | Very high | comic / satirical | memes, economics, platform behavior |
| Caliban | Variable | earthy -> lyrical | grievance, power, sensory language |
| Stephano | High | drunken / boastful | gaming, status, social slang |
| Ariel | Low | precise / uncanny | audio, invisibility, information |

## 9. Rhetorical-Figure Preservation Layer

Identify and preserve, where feasible:

- antithesis

- anaphora

- epistrophe

- parallelism

- chiasmus

- catalogue

- rhetorical question

- apostrophe

- oxymoron

- paradox

- pun

- repetition

- gradatio/climax

- stichomythia

- rhyme

- caesura

Rhetorical fidelity is scored independently from lexical fidelity. A modern phrase may be semantically approximate but still be acceptable if it preserves a crucial rhetorical action and the semantic loss is documented.

## 10. Multiaxial Equivalence Scoring

The former strong/moderate/loose judgment remains available as a summary label, but major interventions should be scored on separate axes:

| Dimension | Score range | Interpretive target |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Semantic fidelity | 0-5 | Literal/propositional correspondence |
| Dramatic-function fidelity | 0-5 | Preservation of scene function |
| Character-voice fidelity | 0-5 | Fit to speaker idiolect |
| Figurative fidelity | 0-5 | Preservation of metaphor/image logic |
| Rhetorical fidelity | 0-5 | Preservation of formal rhetorical action |
| Prosodic fidelity | 0-5 | Stress, rhythm, sound, meter, performability |
| Contemporary authenticity | 0-5 | Plausible current usage |
| Ambiguity preservation | 0-5 | Retention of meaningful multiplicity |

A score is an editorial diagnostic, not a mathematical proof of literary quality.

## 11. Back-Translation / Information-Retention Test

After a heavily modernized passage is drafted, ask:

If a reader knew only the contemporary translation, could that reader reconstruct the major propositions, relationships, emotional stakes, and rhetorical movement of the Shakespearean source?

If the answer is materially no, the passage must be revised or the loss explicitly justified.

## 12. Nuance Loss Register

Known losses are documented rather than hidden. Record at minimum:

| Original feature | Modern treatment | Residual loss |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mythological reference | Modern cultural analogue | Classical association may be reduced |
| Sexual pun | Modern double entendre | Some lexical/anatomical ambiguity may be lost |
| Feudal title distinction | Social-status vocabulary | Historical hierarchy may be compressed |
