
Known losses are documented rather than hidden. Record at minimum:

| Original feature | Modern treatment | Residual loss |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mythological reference | Modern cultural analogue | Classical association may be reduced |
| Sexual pun | Modern double entendre | Some lexical/anatomical ambiguity may be lost |
| Feudal title distinction | Social-status vocabulary | Historical hierarchy may be compressed |
| Theological implication | Retain / gloss / analogize | Document any doctrinal narrowing |
| Legal-historical term | Modern legal metaphor | Technical historical nuance may be reduced |

## 13. Slang Half-Life Rating

Every significant slang term may be assigned a durability class:

| Class | Meaning | Examples / treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | Broadly established contemporary vernacular | ghost, red flag, receipts, vibe |
| B | Strongly current but potentially generational | delulu, rizz, cooked |
| C | Highly moment-specific / meme-dependent | Use deliberately; flag for future historical note |
| D | Project-derived construction | nun-core, we ride at moonrise |

This classification supports both a fixed 2026 historical edition and any later rolling contemporary edition.

## 14. Iconic-Line Threshold

An iconic line may remain substantially intact when:

1. its vocabulary remains largely understandable;

2. its rhythm or phrasing carries exceptional cultural significance;

3. replacing it would destroy more than it gains;

4. surrounding modernization supplies sufficient contemporary contrast.

Slang density is not itself a quality metric.

## 15. Dramatic-Irony Protection Rule

The translation must not accidentally give a character knowledge that the character does not possess.

For each materially ironic passage, record:

- Speaker knowledge state.

- Audience knowledge state.

- Other-character knowledge state when relevant.

- Whether the modern vocabulary risks collapsing those distinctions.

A term such as 'gaslighting' may be used by narration/annotation or a character who recognizes manipulation, but must not be inserted into a character's speech if it would prematurely reveal knowledge unavailable to that character.

## 16. Power / Consent / Coercion Fidelity Check

Mandatory for passages involving marriage, sexuality, enslavement, parental authority, monarchy, violence, racial hierarchy, economic dependency, or legal coercion.

The modernization must not make coercive relationships sound casually consensual or convert ownership, compulsion, threat, or dependency into cute relationship language.

Relationship slang such as ship, hard launch, rizz, down bad, or green/red flag must be checked against actual agency and power conditions before use.

## 17. Performance Test

Every major scene should undergo a read-aloud/performance-oriented review.

| Performance property | Required question |
| --- | --- |
| Breath | Can an actor speak the line naturally? |
| Stress | Do slang words fall on useful beats? |
| Pace | Does rhythm accelerate or slow appropriately? |
| Punch line | Does comic timing land correctly? |
| Emotional turn | Can an actor feel the beat change? |
| Interruption | Does dialogue retain stage timing and overlap potential? |
| Memorability | Does the line have a repeatable verbal shape? |

A line that reads cleverly but cannot be performed naturally does not satisfy the protocol.

## 18. Scene Beat Mapping

Before or during translation, divide the scene into dramatic beats and verify that the adaptation preserves their order, escalation, and turning points.

Example structure:

Iago hesitates -> Othello presses -> Iago warns -> jealousy seeded -> Othello resists -> proof standard declared -> Iago escalates.

Beat mapping is especially important when lexical creativity risks flattening escalation.

## 19. Modernization Intensity Scale

Passages may receive an M0-M5 intensity designation:

| Level | Treatment |
| --- | --- |
| M0 | Essentially Shakespeare intact |
| M1 | Minimal lexical modernization |
| M2 | Selected contemporary substitutions |
| M3 | Romeo-standard integrated modernization |
| M4 | Aggressive slang reconstruction |
| M5 | Experimental / high-density transformation |

M3 remains the default minimum overall target for a Romeo-protocol adaptation, but an individual iconic speech may intentionally operate at M1-M2 while comic or highly colloquial material rises to M4-M5.

Intensity is therefore allocated by dramatic function, not by a quota of slang.

## 20. Pun / Wordplay Reconstruction Requirement

For each significant pun or wordplay mechanism, record:

Original lexical mechanism -> original double meaning -> possible modern double meaning -> resulting substitution.

If no viable contemporary pun exists, retain or gloss the original and record the failure to reproduce the wordplay rather than silently deleting it.

## 21. Cultural-Reference Transformation Matrix

Cultural, mythological, theological, legal, scientific, and historical references must be assigned one treatment:

| Treatment | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Retain | Keep the original reference intact. |
| Gloss | Retain and explain. |
| Modernize | Replace with a contemporary reference of similar function. |
| Analogize | Use a functional modern analogy while preserving the original in annotation. |
| Hybridize | Keep the original reference and add a modern conceptual layer. |

The choice should be driven by dramatic function, cultural load, intelligibility, and loss risk.

## 22. Lexical Saturation Control

Default rule: no more than one primary slang payload per rhetorical unit unless character voice, comic structure, or deliberate escalation justifies stacking.

High-density exceptions may include Bottom, the Fool, Stephano/Trinculo, certain Mercutio passages, or other explicitly comic/escalatory contexts.

Tragic and lyrical passages should be protected from unintended meme parody.

## 25. Applicability Beyond Shakespeare

The v2.0 method may be applied to other historically distant dramatic or literary texts when the user explicitly places them under the protocol. The central transferable unit is dramatic/rhetorical function rather than slang substitution.

Potential domains include Marlowe, Jonson, Milton, Chaucer, Greek tragedy, Restoration drama, opera libretti, Biblical translation experiments, and other historically distant texts.

Genre-specific adaptations may add constraints but may not silently remove the baseline Romeo requirements.

## 26. Version-Control and Adoption Rule

This document is a v2.0 draft candidate. Its creation does not itself erase or invalidate prior artifacts. Existing Romeo-protocol translations remain valid historical products under the version in force when created.

If the user later authorizes v2.0 as controlling, future translations should use the entire v2.0 protocol, and older scenes may be retro-audited without silently rewriting their historical versions.

Any v2.1 or later successor should be cumulative by default and preserve the complete v2.0 content unless the user explicitly authorizes identified deletion, replacement, consolidation, or deprecation.
