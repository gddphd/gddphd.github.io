The review praised the protocol's lexical systematization and character-sensitive register design, while identifying two principal weaknesses: prosodic friction when contemporary slang is forced into blank-verse structures, and tonal deflation when meme-heavy language is applied to high tragedy.

The Gemini review specifically recommended: (1) syntactic reconstruction around the natural phonetic stress of modern vocabulary rather than forcing rigid slang into inherited iambic footprints; and (2) heavier use of conceptually modern digital-era accommodations in tragic material where highly colloquial slang would reduce philosophical or emotional weight.

## 30. Module A - Prosodic Reconstruction Framework

Purpose: prevent semantically successful contemporary vocabulary from producing metrically forced, phonetically unnatural, or theatrically unperformable verse.

### 30.1 Governing Prosodic Priority

Natural contemporary pronunciation -> rhetorical stress -> five-beat architecture -> syllable-count approximation.

A translator must not distort a contemporary term's normal stress, swallow syllables, split a compound unnaturally, or impose an artificial pronunciation merely to preserve an inherited scansion.

### 30.2 Prosodic Reconstruction Pipeline

| Stage | Required operation | Pass condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| P1. Source prosody map | Identify dominant stresses, caesurae, repetitions, rhetorical accelerations, pauses, and any significant metrical departures in Shakespeare's line or unit. | The dramatic rhythm of the source is described before modernization. |
| P2. Contemporary stress map | Mark the natural spoken stresses of proposed slang, compounds, acronyms, and digital-era expressions. | No pronunciation is invented merely for meter. |
| P3. Friction screen | Check polysyllabic load, consonant clusters, adjacent spondees, awkward unstressed runs, and phrases requiring syllabic swallowing. | The candidate can be spoken naturally. |
| P4. Syntax rebuild | When friction appears, reconstruct the entire connective syntax around the contemporary phrase rather than forcing the phrase into the old syntax. | Natural speech and rhetorical function both survive. |
| P5. Five-beat reconstruction | Re-establish a performable stress architecture that trends toward five beats where the scene and source support it. | Beat structure arises from speech rather than mechanical syllable counting. |
| P6. Rhetorical-momentum check | Verify that imperatives, lists, antitheses, repetitions, climaxes, and pauses still drive forward. | The modern line has comparable dramatic propulsion. |
| P7. Actor read-aloud test | Speak the line at performance pace without explanatory distortion. | No forced stress, swallowed syllable, or involuntary halt is required. |
| P8. Record outcome | Assign prosodic-fidelity and performance results; document any residual friction. | Residual compromise is explicit rather than hidden. |

### 30.3 Phonotactic Friction Check

Flag a candidate expression when it creates one or more of the following:

- dense consonant clustering that interrupts an otherwise flowing verse line;

- multiple adjacent strong stresses that produce an unintended halt or hammering effect;

- a long polysyllabic item that can fit only by unnatural compression;

- stress assignment inconsistent with ordinary 2026 pronunciation;

- a caesura introduced by lexical awkwardness rather than dramatic intention;

- a phrase whose natural rhythm shifts attention away from the dramatic target.

### 30.4 Full-Syntax Reconstruction Rule

When a contemporary term is dramatically valuable but metrically incompatible with the inherited sentence, rebuild the complete syntactic unit around that term.

The protocol prefers a genuinely performable new line with Shakespearean rhetorical momentum over a line that preserves old syntax while mis-stressing new language.

### 30.5 Prosodic Veto

A slang term may be rejected even when its semantic and contemporary-authenticity scores are high if its natural phonetics materially damage breath, stress, pace, rhetorical momentum, or actor intelligibility.

### 30.6 Expanded Prosodic Scoring

| Dimension | 0-5 diagnostic question |
| --- | --- |
| Natural-pronunciation fidelity | Can the term be spoken exactly as contemporary speakers normally pronounce it? |
| Beat integration | Does the modern phrase participate naturally in the reconstructed beat pattern? |
| Phonotactic fluency | Do consonants, syllables, and stress transitions remain performable? |
| Rhetorical momentum | Does the line retain its forward dramatic force? |
| Breath / caesura integrity | Are pauses playable and dramatically motivated? |
| Actor performability | Can an actor deliver the line at pace without compensatory distortion? |

## 31. Module B - Dramatic Gravity and Tonal Fidelity Framework

Purpose: prevent contemporary authenticity or lexical cleverness from flattening tragedy, catharsis, awe, terror, grief, or philosophical seriousness.

### 31.1 Dramatic Gravity Classification

| Gravity | Dramatic condition | Default slang tolerance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| G1 - Comic | Farce, banter, clowning, deliberate vulgarity, comic escalation. | Very high |
| G2 - Social / conversational | Courtship, rivalry, ordinary conflict, exposition, friendly mockery. | High |
| G3 - Serious | Consequential argument, emotional conflict, political negotiation, moral confrontation. | Moderate |
| G4 - Tragic | Betrayal, death, psychological collapse, irreversible loss, severe coercion. | Low to moderate |
| G5 - Sublime / catastrophic | Existential, metaphysical, apocalyptic, sacred, visionary, or intensely lyrical material. | Very low |

### 31.2 Contemporary Lexicon Tiers

| Tier | Register type | Illustrative vocabulary | Preferred use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A - Meme / youth slang | Highly colloquial, socially marked, often comic or generational. | rizz, delulu, cooked, GOAT, big yikes, ratioed | Comedy, youth dialogue, mockery, informal beats. |
| B - Broad contemporary vernacular | Widely understandable current colloquial language. | ghost, receipts, red flag, vibe, locked in, no cap | Broadly usable but still character- and gravity-dependent. |
| C - Digital-conceptual metaphor | Modern systems/platform language with lower automatic comic load. | hard reset, admin access, deplatformed, unverified, hard delete | Serious or tragic passages when the conceptual analogy retains weight. |
| D - Modern standard-English reconstruction | Contemporary syntax and vocabulary without a slang quota. | plain contemporary diction | Tragic, sublime, philosophical, or lyrical passages where slang would trivialize the moment. |

### 31.3 Gravity-to-Lexicon Guidance

| Gravity | Preferred tiers | Typical intensity ceiling | Editorial note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| G1 | A-D | M4-M5 | Stacking may be justified by comic escalation or character idiolect. |
| G2 | A-D | M3-M4 | Maintain character differentiation and avoid novelty for novelty's sake. |
| G3 | B-D; selective A | M2-M3 | Slang must not distract from consequential stakes. |
| G4 | C-D; selective B | M1-M3 | Protect grief, terror, betrayal, and irreversible consequence from meme deflation. |
| G5 | D; selective C | M0-M2 | Preserve awe, philosophical weight, lyricism, and iconic phrasing unless modernization clearly improves function. |

### 31.4 Tonal-Deflation Gate

Does this contemporary phrase cause the audience to laugh, detach, recognize the meme, or admire the cleverness at a moment when Shakespeare requires fear, grief, pity, horror, moral pressure, or awe?

If yes, the substitution normally fails the gate regardless of semantic cleverness, contemporary authenticity, or low overall slang density.

### 31.5 Single-Term Deflation Rule

Lexical saturation and tonal deflation are separate problems. A tragic passage can be damaged by a single highly comic or meme-dependent term even when the rest of the passage is restrained.

### 31.6 Catharsis and Sublime-Language Protection

For G4-G5 material, preserve emotional amplitude before pursuing slang density. The protocol should prefer Tier C digital-conceptual accommodation or Tier D contemporary reconstruction when Tier A-B language would make suffering, metaphysical terror, grief, or revelation feel socially trivial.

### 31.7 Character and Beat Override

Gravity applies at the beat level, not only the scene level. A Fool, clown, servant, or drunk may sustain a higher lexical tier within a tragic scene, while the tragic protagonist's adjacent speech may require a much lower modernization intensity.

### 31.8 Tonal Fidelity Scoring

| Dimension | 0-5 diagnostic question |
| --- | --- |
| Gravity alignment | Does the lexical register fit the dramatic gravity of the beat? |
| Catharsis preservation | Does the language preserve rather than deflate pity, fear, grief, or moral consequence? |
| Comic-detachment risk | Does the term introduce unintended distance or meme recognition? (5 = no inappropriate detachment) |
| Sublime / philosophical integrity | Does the line retain conceptual and emotional scale? |
| Character-tonal fit | Does this speaker plausibly carry this register in this moment? |
